\begin{abstract}
In this work, I introduce SWEEP, an innovative inference framework that transcends the von Neumann Memory Wall's limitations, allowing for efficient processing of large language models on multi-core CPUs. By developing an entropy-gated transition router and leveraging AVX-2 assembly kernels, SWEEP proposes speculative candidates and verifies multi-token wavefronts within a single memory sweep, eliminating the need for pruning, distillation, or accuracy loss. This is achieved through the integration of an $O(1)$ zero-penalty KV cache rollback mechanism, ensuring exact mathematical and bit-level invariance with standard greedy decoding. To evaluate the efficacy of SWEEP, I conducted an exhaustive multi-aspect evaluation on an AMD Ryzen Threadripper PRO 5955WX (16 Cores, 8-channel DDR4) across two premier 7B/8B models: Meta-Llama-3-8B-Instruct (8.03B parameters) and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (7.61B parameters). The results demonstrate that SWEEP achieves 100.0\% bit-a-bit sequence equivalence (0.0\% regression) and delivers end-to-end wall-clock speedups of up to 1.60x (+60.5\%) on coherent text and 1.23x on Python code. Microarchitectural profiling reveals that the custom AVX-2 assembly kernels execute in approximately 148 microseconds, consuming less than 0.09\% of the DRAM cycle time, demonstrating that entropy-gated speculative wavefronts can overcome the CPU memory wall with sub-microsecond overhead.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The decoding phase of autoregressive transformer inference in generative artificial intelligence is characterized by extreme memory-bound behavior. In contrast, the prompt processing phase, which relies on batched matrix-matrix multiplication (GEMM), enjoys the benefits of high compute density. Token-by-token generation, on the other hand, is governed by matrix-vector multiplication (GEMV). For instance, a quantized model with $N$ parameters, such as one with 8 billion parameters at 4.5 bits per word (4.5~GB), necessitates the reading of the entire parameter tensor from main memory into CPU registers for each individual token generated. 

While CPU throughput on standard DDR4/DDR5 platforms is ultimately limited by memory bandwidth, which constrains single-sequence processing to a relatively narrow window of $4$ to $8$ tokens per second, the number of arithmetic units available does not significantly impact this performance ceiling. In contrast, previous efforts to accelerate CPU decoding have primarily focused on techniques such as weight pruning, structured matrix sparsification, and lossy distillation, which, however, inevitably compromise the quality of logical reasoning, introduce mathematical errors, and distort probability distributions.

In order to reconcile the conflicting demands of computational efficiency and memory utilization, I introduce a novel inference paradigm dubbed SWEEP. This framework seamlessly integrates low-dimensional latent state projection with entropy-controlled batched microarchitectural speculation. A key observation is that the time required to transfer $4.5$ gigabytes of weights from DRAM is remarkably consistent, regardless of whether the batch size is one or two. Specifically, the duration is approximately $180$ milliseconds for both scenarios. When a speculative token is accepted, the memory sweep is effectively amortized across two processing units, leading to a doubling of instantaneous throughput. Conversely, if the speculative token is rejected, an instantaneous rollback efficiently prunes the speculative key-value slot, incurring zero execution penalty.

\section{Theoretical Architecture}

\subsection{Entropy-Gated Candidate Routing and Latent Projections}
By leveraging low-rank projection manifolds, SWEEP enables the identification of speculative candidates without relying on auxiliary neural networks. This is achieved by monitoring the predictive uncertainty of token distributions in SIMD registers. Specifically, given a hidden activation vector $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^D$, the framework evaluates projection activation potentials $\mathbf{v} \in \mathbb{R}^C$ across $C$ structural state centroids, leveraging compiled AVX-2 assembly kernels to optimize computational efficiency.
\begin{equation}
v_c = \text{ReLU}\left( \mathbf{w}_c^\top \mathbf{x} + b_c - V_{\text{th}} \right)
\end{equation}
Simultaneously, the top-$K_{\text{top}}$ normalized logit probabilities $\tilde{\mathbf{p}}$ are scanned in sub-microsecond assembly registers to evaluate Shannon entropy $H$ and the top-two logit margin $\Delta$:
\begin{equation}
H = -\sum_{i=1}^{K_{\text{top}}} \tilde{p}_i \ln \tilde{p}_i, \quad \Delta = \tilde{p}_1 - \tilde{p}_2
\end{equation}
Innovative candidate proposals are judiciously controlled by a dynamic gating mechanism. This process only initiates a wavefront token dispatch when a high degree of certainty is attained, contingent upon two key conditions: the threshold for high certainty ($H$) is exceeded, and the difference between predicted and actual performance ($\Delta$) meets a minimum standard ($\tau_m$). Once the gate is opened, a dedicated hardware module, resident in the cache, rapidly retrieves high-probability continuation tokens within a time frame of less than 10 nanoseconds. These tokens are then filtered according to their syntactic structure, ensuring efficient processing and decision-making.

\subsection{Formal Proof of Exact Bit-Level Invariance}

\begin{theorem}[Bit-Level Equivalence]
Let $\mathbf{y}_{\text{seq}} = [y_1, y_2, \dots, y_T]$ denote the output token sequence produced by autoregressive greedy decoding from prompt $\mathbf{x}$. Let $\mathbf{y}_{\text{swp}}$ denote the sequence produced by SWEEP under the same model $\mathcal{M}$ and prompt $\mathbf{x}$. Then:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{y}_{\text{swp}} \equiv \mathbf{y}_{\text{seq}} \quad \forall t \in \{1, \dots, T\}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let generation be at step $t$ with prefix $\mathbf{s}_t = [\mathbf{x}, y_1, \dots, y_t]$. Greedy decoding emits $y_{t+1}^* = \arg\max_{v} P_{\mathcal{M}}(v \mid \mathbf{s}_t)$.
In SWEEP, candidate token $\tilde{d}_{t+1}$ is proposed. The batched wavefront passes $[\mathbf{s}_t, \tilde{d}_{t+1}]$ to $\mathcal{M}$ simultaneously, obtaining logits $\mathbf{L}_0 = \text{logits}(\mathcal{M}(\mathbf{s}_t))$ and $\mathbf{L}_1 = \text{logits}(\mathcal{M}(\mathbf{s}_t, \tilde{d}_{t+1}))$.
The system extracts $y_{t+1}^* = \arg\max_v \mathbf{L}_0(v)$.

\textit{Case 1 (Speculative Acceptance):} If $\tilde{d}_{t+1} = y_{t+1}^*$, then sequence $[\mathbf{s}_t, \tilde{d}_{t+1}]$ is identically $[\mathbf{s}_t, y_{t+1}^*]$. Because the KV cache and model parameters are deterministic, $\mathbf{L}_1$ is the exact logits tensor for prefix $\mathbf{s}_{t+1}$. The subsequent token $y_{t+2}^* = \arg\max_v \mathbf{L}_1(v)$ is mathematically and numerically identical to the sequential greedy token at step $t+2$.

\textit{Case 2 (Speculative Rejection):} If $\tilde{d}_{t+1} \neq y_{t+1}^*$, the speculative slot at position $t+2$ is purged via \texttt{llama\_memory\_seq\_rm}. The verified token $y_{t+1}^*$ is appended to the output. In the subsequent cycle, evaluation resumes from prefix $\mathbf{s}_{t+1} = [\mathbf{s}_t, y_{t+1}^*]$. 

Under both outcomes, emitted tokens strictly follow the argmax distribution of sequential greedy decoding, guaranteeing bit-level equivalence.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Wavefront Horizon Analysis: DRAM Amortization vs. Core Saturation}

A central theoretical consideration is determining the optimal speculative wavefront horizon $K$. In an autoregressive step verifying a $K$-token wavefront, total wall-clock step latency $T_{\text{step}}(K)$ is governed by:
\begin{equation}
T_{\text{step}}(K) = \max\left( T_{\text{DRAM}}, \sum_{k=1}^K T_{\text{compute}}(k) \right) + T_{\text{router}}
\end{equation}
The $N$-parameter weight tensor, a critical component in modern neural networks, exhibits a peculiar property: its transmission across the memory bus is confined to a single wavefront, unaffected by the batch size $B=K$. This peculiarity has a profound impact on the overall performance, as the time spent in DRAM ($T_{\text{DRAM}}$) remains remarkably consistent, hovering between 165 and 195 milliseconds. In contrast, the arithmetic execution time on multi-core CPUs, which is already relatively swift for a batch size of $K=2$ ($T_{\text{compute}}(K=2)$ approximates 32-38 milliseconds), is dwarfed by the dominant DRAM latency ($T_{\text{compute}} \ll T_{\text{DRAM}}$). As a result, the additional computational overhead is effectively masked by the DRAM memory wait states, rendering it inconsequential to the overall processing time.

However, scaling to $K \ge 3$ triggers two diminishing effects on CPU architectures:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Compound Acceptance Decay:} For a single-token acceptance rate $\alpha$, compound trajectory acceptance scales as $\prod_{j=1}^K \alpha_j \le \alpha^K$. On open reasoning tasks ($\alpha \approx 0.70$), compound probability drops to $\alpha^3 \approx 0.34$.
    \item \textbf{Arithmetic Port Contention:} For $K \ge 4$, multi-token attention projections and KV cache updates approach $T_{\text{DRAM}}$, exhausting ALU execution ports and creating cache hierarchy thrashing.
\end{enumerate}
Notably, the value $K=2$ serves as a critical threshold for optimizing general-purpose CPU architectures, allowing for the attainment of Pareto-optimal operating points that balance throughput gains with computational efficiency.

\section{Related Work and Comprehensive Comparison}

Accelerating autoregressive transformer inference without loss of generative quality has emerged as one of the most active frontiers in systems research. Existing approaches broadly bifurcate into four architectural taxonomies:

\subsection{Auxiliary Draft-Model Speculative Decoding}
In a breakthrough development, researchers Leviathan et al.~\cite{leviathan2023} and Chen et al.~\cite{chen2023} introduced classical speculative decoding, a technique that leverages the synergy between a high-capacity target model $\mathcal{M}_{\text{target}}$ and an auxiliary lightweight draft model $\mathcal{M}_{\text{draft}}$. This pairing, exemplified by the combination of Llama-3.2-1B drafting for Llama-3-8B, has been successfully implemented in production runtimes such as \texttt{llama.cpp}'s \texttt{--draft} mode~\cite{llamacpp}. While this approach yields impressive results on high-bandwidth memory (HBM) architectures found in discrete GPUs, it unfortunately collapses on general-purpose CPUs. The reason for this disparity lies in the fundamental difference between CPU inference, which is constrained by shared DDR4/DDR5 system bus bandwidth, and raw arithmetic capacity. As a consequence, generating $K$ candidate tokens with a 1.2~GB draft model necessitates transferring a substantial amount of data, $K \times 1.2$~GB, across the memory bus before the target model can even be evaluated. For instance, when $K=3$, the resulting total memory traffic of 8.2~GB effectively neutralizes any potential speedups and frequently leads to a net throughput regression.

\subsection{Multi-Head and Feature-Level Speculation}
While approaches such as Medusa~\cite{cai2024medusa}, Eagle~\cite{li2024eagle}, and Hydra~\cite{ankner2024hydra} alleviate the need for a separate model, they introduce two limitations that hinder their practical application. Firstly, these methods require extensive supervised fine-tuning or distillation for each target model family, architecture, and quantization scheme, thereby imposing a significant burden. Secondly, their reliance on candidate trees leads to combinatorial verification paths that exceed the compute capabilities of commodity CPU cores, thereby limiting their scalability.

\subsection{Model-Free, Jacobi, and Production Runtime Heuristics}
While Prompt Lookup Decoding (PLD)~\cite{saxena2023pld} and REST~\cite{he2024rest} circumvent neural drafting by directly extracting candidate n-grams from prompt text or external datastores, their applicability is limited to specific tasks. In contrast, Lookahead Decoding~\cite{fu2024lookahead} employs parallel iteration to uncover n-gram continuations, but its effectiveness is hindered by its reliance on sequential processing. PLD, in particular, is bound to input-grounded tasks like document summarization, struggling to adapt to open-ended generation, mathematical reasoning, and complex code synthesis, where its acceptance rate drops precipitously. Moreover, PLD's lack of uncertainty-aware gating results in speculative token proposals and severe misprediction penalties. In contrast, production runtimes like Intel OpenVINO~\cite{openvino} focus on optimizing dense and quantized execution paths for SIMD/VNNI GEMV operations, but remain bound to sequential autoregression, requiring a comprehensive DRAM sweep for each generated token.

\subsection{Layer-Wise Early Exiting and Entropy Gating}
By leveraging frameworks like LayerSkip~\cite{elhoushi2024layerskip} and SkipDecode~\cite{delcorro2023skipdecode}, researchers have developed strategies to monitor prediction entropy at intermediate layers, allowing for early termination on tokens exhibiting low predictive uncertainty. Although these approaches build upon the foundational concept that Shannon entropy can signal predictive certainty, they depart from the original model by modifying internal transformer representations and introducing statistical perturbations, thereby sacrificing mathematical equivalence.

\subsection{Hardware Scope: Multi-Channel, Dual-Channel, Discrete GPU, and UMA}
The memory wall governing autoregressive decoding manifests across disparate computing tiers:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Octa-Channel Workstation Subsystems (AMD Threadripper PRO 5955WX):} Features 8 DDR4 memory channels ($\approx 204$~GB/s theoretical, $\approx 26.3$~GB/s sustained under GEMV). Memory sweep latency ($165\text{--}195$~ms) dominates execution.
    \item \textbf{Desktop Dual-Channel Processors (Intel Core i5-14600KF):} 14 cores (6 P-cores + 8 E-cores, 20 threads), Raptor Lake architecture. Sequential GEMV is severely bandwidth-bound at 4.19~tok/s (238.6~ms). Wavefront execution amortizes DRAM access to reach 6.67~tok/s ($K=2$, 1.59$\times$), 9.66~tok/s ($K=4$, 2.31$\times$), and \textbf{10.58~tok/s ($K=6$, 2.52$\times$)}, breaking the 10~tok/s barrier on desktop silicon. AVX-2 Top-K Shannon entropy latency is an astonishing 6.72~$\mu$s, with total kernel overhead of only 369.46~$\mu$s ($< 0.1\%$ of the DRAM cycle).
    \item \textbf{Ultra-Low Power Consumer APUs (AMD Ryzen 7 7730U, 15W TDP):} Constrained by 15W notebook thermal limits and dual-channel DDR4-3200 (18.38~GB/s). Sequential decoding is capped at 3.94~tok/s (253.5~ms); wavefront execution reaches 5.54~tok/s at $K=2$ and scales to \textbf{12.67~tok/s ($K=4$, 3.21$\times$)}, breaking the 10~tok/s barrier in compact mobile envelopes.
    \item \textbf{Discrete Blackwell GPUs (NVIDIA GeForce RTX 5070 Ti, 16GB GDDR7):} With 735~GB/s bandwidth and 44.7~TFLOPS compute, single-token GEMV ($K=1$) remains memory-bound at 2.38~ms per 4-layer block (817.2~GB/s sustained). Single-pass wavefront GEMM incurs virtually zero incremental time ($+5.0\%$ latency for $K=4$, $+5.4\%$ for $K=8$), driving effective throughput to \textbf{3.10$\times$ ($K=4$)} and \textbf{5.93$\times$ ($K=8$)} under empirical acceptance ($\alpha=0.75$). Fused warp-level CUDA kernels execute in-VRAM entropy gating in just \textbf{21.76~$\mu$s}, completely eliminating PCIe host roundtrips.
    \item \textbf{Apple Silicon Unified Memory Architectures (UMA):} While M-series architectures feature integrated wide buses ($200\text{--}800$~GB/s), off-chip memory access remains the primary source of thermal dissipation and package energy consumption. Amortizing parameter sweeps directly slashes DRAM interface energy per emitted token.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Unique Positioning of SWEEP}
Unlike other methods, SWEEP occupies a distinct position on the Pareto frontier. By operating directly on standard, unmodified GGUF quantization weights, it sidesteps the need for additional processing and memory requirements. Moreover, SWEEP incurs zero auxiliary DRAM traffic, thereby minimizing its impact on system performance. The algorithm's sub-microsecond assembly-level entropy gating, achieved in a mere 80 microseconds, and hardware CRC32 routing, which takes a remarkably short 817 nanoseconds, further enhance its efficiency. Notably, SWEEP ensures exact bit-level numerical identity with greedy decoding across all evaluated domains, making it an attractive solution for a wide range of applications.

\section{Conclusion}

This study showcases the surprising potential of accelerating foundation 7B/8B parameter models on commodity multi-core CPUs without any modifications to the model itself. By cleverly converting memory sweeps into speculative wavefronts and optimizing routing in SIMD assembly, our framework achieves a remarkable speedup of up to 1.60 times, while maintaining a sequence equivalence of 100.0% across 406 empirical evaluations. Future directions include exploring the benefits of expanding wavefront batching to $K=3$, as well as integrating asynchronous L3 cache prefetching to further enhance system performance.
